# Patterns from the field research (Voltia, Team 3)

Source numbers (#1 to #8) are those of `research/field_notes.md`. Quotes are the translations written there.

## P1. Chasing for news: the client calls and learns nothing
- **Verbatim:** "I phone every other day to ask, they always tell me the same thing and give me no solution" (#2). "They are constantly contacting us because these processes take a long time" (#1).
- **Job to be done:** When my file is open, I want to know where it is and what happens next, so I can stop asking.
- **Insight:** The wait itself is bearable; not knowing is what pushes clients to call again and again, and the installer's office absorbs those calls.

## P2. Nobody owns the file: retailer and distributor point at each other
- **Verbatim:** "The retailer says the distributor rejects the request, and the distributor says the retailer does" (#4). "My retailer Iberdrola washes its hands of it and blames Eléctrica Conquense" (#3).
- **Job to be done:** When something blocks my file, I want to know who is responsible, so I can ask the right person.
- **Insight:** Clients do not understand who does what, and the answer changes depending on who they call.

## P3. Paperwork loops: rejected documents, missing signatures, return visits
- **Verbatim:** "This time, the fourth time they send them, they are rejected again" (#5). "Sometimes the client forgets to sign some papers and you have to chase him" (#1). Four or five return visits per month for a photo or a paper (#1).
- **Job to be done:** When a document is needed, I want to know exactly what and by when, so the file does not stop because of me.
- **Insight:** Many stops are small and avoidable (a signature, a photo, a URL that cannot be read), and both client and installer cause them.

## P4. Nobody can say how long it will take
- **Verbatim:** "Some files take two years and some take two weeks. It depends on what has to be done" (#1). "In 2023 i-DE activated 59% of the files in under five days" and "only 2%" took more than 60 days (#7). "15 installations that have been in process for 15 months" (#8).
- **Job to be done:** When I start a file, I want a realistic idea of the time, so I can plan my life or my business around it.
- **Insight:** The range is huge, so most clients end up in the fast majority while a few are stuck for months; the client cannot tell beforehand which one they are.

## P5. Trust comes from a known person who is open and delivers
- **Verbatim:** "Mostly through contacts and recommendations" (#1). "We tell him the file may take long, and we try to be very transparent about it" (#1). "They did it in the agreed time and budget... Congratulations Marc, we will count on you for the next projects" (#6).
- **Job to be done:** When I hand my project to an installer, I want to trust a person I know, so I do not feel alone against the paperwork.
- **Insight:** A named contact and honest warnings build trust. Complaints are not followed up (#1), so doubt has no channel to reach the office.
