How it works

  1. Goal

    Three personas, one per point of the research line, each passing the checker, plus a report that compares them. Only the research as input: no web, no invented data, no solutions.

  2. Brain

    Claude, running headless in Claude Code. It chooses its next step on its own.

  3. Tools

    Read the research, write files, and run check_persona.py: sections in order, all five patterns used, every section traced to a pattern, invented details flagged, no solutions, one page.

  4. Memory

    A step log it writes as it works. Think, act, observe, reflect: every decision is on the record below.

Hard end · A file that is very slow or blocked

Julio* - "Everything is done perfectly and I cannot have electricity because of this paperwork."

“”

PASS435 wordschecked by check_persona.py

Profile

Homeowner waiting for a new supply or a power increase*. Age and job are not in the research*. He writes in electricity forums, sometimes at 2 a.m. He knows the legal deadline (30 working days) exactly.

Background

His electrical work is finished. His file is stopped, rejected or in a loop. Some people in his position have waited since 03/10/2023 or "6 months". He did not choose the company that owns the file. He chose an installer, maybe through a contact*.

Goals

  • Get the power connected and live normally again.
  • Know why the file is stopped and who must act.
  • Stop spending hours on it.

Pains and frustrations

  • Calls every other day bring "the same" answer (P1).
  • The retailer blames the distributor, and the distributor blames the retailer (P2).
  • The same papers are rejected a fourth time for a small detail, like a URL that cannot be seen (P3).
  • He lives cold, with a few light bulbs (P5).
  • The work takes "5 hours a day" and he ends desperate.

Jobs to be done

  • Functional: know where the file is and what is missing (P1); get it moving without coordinating it himself (P2); have papers accepted the first time (P3).
  • Personal: get his normal life back and stop feeling powerless (P5).
  • Social: be able to say to his family* that it is not his fault; keep the word he gave them*.

A day in their life during the wait

He calls the distributor in the morning*. They tell him the same. He calls the retailer, who sends him back. He re-sends a document. At night he cannot sleep* and writes in a forum. The next day he repeats it (P1, P2).

How they look for news today

Phone calls every other day, forum posts, and rumours of others who wait longer (P1). He asks strangers whether he can report them, because nobody inside the process answers him (P5).

What makes them trust or doubt an installer

He doubts when the installer is silent or when the delay was born in the job, like a missing signature or photo (P3). He trusts a person he knows by name, as Voltia's clients trust Marc (P4). Complaints are "not usually followed up", so he may feel alone*.

Easy end · Follows many files with ease

Nuria* - "They did it in the agreed time and budget, with a good finish."

“”

PASS471 wordschecked by check_persona.py

Profile

Runs projects in Valencia and follows many jobs and files at once, with a team around her*. She judges a job by time and budget, the two things she must answer for. Her age is not in the research*.

Background

She gave Voltia a project that was hard to do, with very little time. The job went well and she plans to hire Voltia again. She came, or would come, through contacts and recommendations. Her files are many, so she counts them, like the city official with 15 installations.

Goals

  • Keep the promise she made to the people she answers to.
  • Know that each file is moving without checking all of them herself.
  • Work again with the same installer on the next projects.

Pains and frustrations

  • Her files still depend on a process she does not control (P2).
  • A rejection can come weeks after its cause, like an error in a document or one made by the distributor (P3).
  • She does not know if a file will take "two weeks or two years" (P1).
  • When one file is blocked, the finished work cannot be used (P5)*. She has less time for it than a single client*.

Jobs to be done

  • Functional: know where each file is and what comes next (P1); have each one accepted the first time (P3).
  • Personal: feel safe because she knows one person by name (P4).
  • Social: keep her word to the people she answers to, with the time and budget agreed (P4).

A day in their life during the wait

She goes through her jobs in the morning*. For most she needs nothing, because a person she trusts has told her the file may take long, in a transparent way (P4). She calls only for the one that is out of line*. In the evening she tells her own people what to expect* (P1).

How they look for news today

She asks the person she knows, Marc, not the company or the distributor (P4). She does not post in forums*. Clients "constantly contact" Voltia, so she may be one of many callers (P1).

What makes them trust or doubt an installer

She trusts the kept promise: agreed time, agreed budget, good finish, and a person to thank by name (P4). She would doubt after a promise broken by someone she trusted, or a delay that began in the job, such as a missing signature or photo (P3). If the work is done but power is not live, trust falls (P5)*.

Regular · A normal file

Carmen* - "I just want to know if it is normal."

“”

PASS452 wordschecked by check_persona.py

Profile

A Voltia client in Valencia* with an ordinary file: a certificate or an Iberdrola procedure*. She is not an expert and not in crisis. Her age, job and home are not in the research*. She came through a contact or a recommendation.

Background

Voltia told her at the start that the file may take long, and tried to be "very transparent". Files last "two weeks or two years", so she does not know where hers will fall (P1). The research has no direct voice of a client in this middle, so most of her details are inferred*.

Goals

  • Have the file finished in a time she can plan around*.
  • Understand what the installer needs from her, like a signature (P3).
  • Feel that someone is looking at her file (P4).

Pains and frustrations

  • She cannot tell if her wait is normal. Averages from i-DE (93% of solar files under 20 days) are not her case (P1).
  • She may forget to sign papers, and nobody warns her at the right time (P3).
  • The team may need to return to the site for a missing photo, four or five times a month (P3).
  • Her file belongs to Iberdrola, not to Voltia, but Voltia is the face she knows (P2).

Jobs to be done

  • Functional: know where my file is, what is missing and what comes next (P1).
  • Personal: feel safe because she knows one person by name (P4); avoid the helplessness of the hard end (P5)*.
  • Social: be able to tell her family* that the work is on track.

A day in their life during the wait

She does not think about the file most days*. A silence of some weeks makes her wonder. She sends a message or calls Voltia, as clients "constantly" do (P1). She signs a paper when asked, and sometimes late (P3).

How they look for news today

She contacts Voltia, probably Marc or Toni (P4). She does not read forums* and does not know the distributor's own process (P2). If the answer is "the same", she calls again (P1).

What makes them trust or doubt an installer

She trusts honesty about delay and a known person, as the happy client trusts Marc (P4). She doubts if complaints are "not usually followed up" and the same mistake repeats (P3). If the wait grows and the work is done, she could move toward the hard end (P5)*.

Nothing is still failing. Two honest limits: persona 2 joins a happy Voltia client (N. M.) with an expert who manages 15 files (Herrera), whose files were slow. Persona 3 has no direct voice in the research, so it is mostly inferred and carries many asterisks.

What the three share (common jobs to be done)

  • Functional: know where my file is, what is missing and what comes next (P1).
  • Functional: have the file accepted the first time (P3).
  • Emotional: feel safe because I know one person by name, and keep my word to the people I answer to (P4).

All three also live with a process that nobody owns for them (P2).

What changes from one end of the line to the other

  • Hard end: the wait is long and the work is finished, so the feeling moves from "when?" to "can I report them?" and helplessness (P5). He manages the file himself, for hours (P2). He calls often and gets "the same" (P1).
  • Regular: the file is not in crisis. The need is to know if the wait is normal, and delays can start in the job itself (P1, P3).
  • Easy end: trust sits in a known person and a kept promise (P4). The need is less about calling and more about knowing each file is on track, though the same process limits still apply (P2, P3).
  • Across the line, P5 grows with the wait, and P4 holds or breaks the trust.

Which persona to design for first

The team should start with persona 1, the hard end. It is the only persona built from real quotes, and it shows all five patterns in their strongest form. The cost of silence is highest there: hours of calls, a home without power, and trust in a person lost. Persona 3 is the likely majority but rests on inference, so it should be checked against real clients afterwards.

The five patterns it worked from

Source: research/field_notes.md only. Quotes are word for word from that file (English translation).

P1 Calls come from silence, not from time

  • Verbatim: Julio21: "I phone every other day to ask; they always tell me the same". Toni: "They are constantly contacting us"; "Some files take two years and some take two weeks."
  • Job to be done (functional): Know where my file is, what is missing and what comes next.
  • Insight: The user cannot tell if his wait is normal. A call that brings back "the same" tells him nobody looks at his file, so he calls again.

P2 Nobody owns the file, so the user becomes its manager

  • Verbatim: pantaleon: "The retailer says the distributor rejects the request, and the distributor says the retailer does"; "5 hours a day". PUMA: "My retailer Iberdrola washes its hands".
  • Job to be done (functional): Get my file moving without coordinating it myself.
  • Insight: Each company answers for one piece and sends him to the other. Only the user sees the whole file.

P3 Delays are born on day one and show up weeks later

  • Verbatim: Tevi62: "the fourth they send them, they are rejected again". Toni: "the client forgets to sign some papers". Herrera: "the distributors' own errors are what bog down the process".
  • Job to be done (functional): Have the file accepted the first time.
  • Insight: The cause (a signature, a format, an error) and the rejection are weeks apart, so the rejection feels random. He cannot learn from it.

P4 Trust is in a person and in a kept promise

  • Verbatim: N. M.: "in the agreed time and budget... Congratulations Marc". Toni: "Marc is the one who deals most with the client"; clients come "through contacts and recommendations".
  • Job to be done (emotional, social and personal): Keep my word to the people I answer to; feel safe because I know one person by name.
  • Insight: The happy client praises the kept promise, not the technique. A delay feels like a promise broken by someone he trusted.

P5 Finished work that cannot be used feels unfair

  • Verbatim: Julio21: "Everything is done perfectly and I cannot have electricity because of this paperwork". PUMA: "cold and with only a few light bulbs"; "Can I report them?". Tevi62: "helplessness".
  • Job to be done (emotional, personal): Get my normal life back and stop feeling powerless.
  • Insight: When the work is visibly done, the wait looks like a decision against him. Posts move from "when?" to "can I report them?" to helplessness.
