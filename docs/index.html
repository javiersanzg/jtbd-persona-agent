What this is

A persona is a fictional user built from real research. The challenge: clients of a small electrical installer in Valencia wait months for their electricity file and do not know what is happening. The agent writes three personas: the worst wait (hard end), the easiest (easy end) and a normal one (regular).

The patterns it found

Written by the agent. In italics, the insight: why it happens.

  1. P1

    Chasing for news: the client calls and learns nothing

    The wait itself is bearable; not knowing is what pushes clients to call again and again, and the installer's office absorbs those calls.

    Job to be done

    When my file is open, I want to know where it is and what happens next, so I can stop asking.

    What people said

    "I phone every other day to ask, they always tell me the same thing and give me no solution" (#2). "They are constantly contacting us because these processes take a long time" (#1).

  2. P2

    Nobody owns the file: retailer and distributor point at each other

    Clients do not understand who does what, and the answer changes depending on who they call.

    Job to be done

    When something blocks my file, I want to know who is responsible, so I can ask the right person.

    What people said

    "The retailer says the distributor rejects the request, and the distributor says the retailer does" (#4). "My retailer Iberdrola washes its hands of it and blames Eléctrica Conquense" (#3).

  3. P3

    Paperwork loops: rejected documents, missing signatures, return visits

    Many stops are small and avoidable (a signature, a photo, a URL that cannot be read), and both client and installer cause them.

    Job to be done

    When a document is needed, I want to know exactly what and by when, so the file does not stop because of me.

    What people said

    "This time, the fourth time they send them, they are rejected again" (#5). "Sometimes the client forgets to sign some papers and you have to chase him" (#1). Four or five return visits per month for a photo or a paper (#1).

  4. P4

    Nobody can say how long it will take

    The range is huge, so most clients end up in the fast majority while a few are stuck for months; the client cannot tell beforehand which one they are.

    Job to be done

    When I start a file, I want a realistic idea of the time, so I can plan my life or my business around it.

    What people said

    "Some files take two years and some take two weeks. It depends on what has to be done" (#1). "In 2023 i-DE activated 59% of the files in under five days" and "only 2%" took more than 60 days (#7). "15 installations that have been in process for 15 months" (#8).

  5. P5

    Trust comes from a known person who is open and delivers

    A named contact and honest warnings build trust. Complaints are not followed up (#1), so doubt has no channel to reach the office.

    Job to be done

    When I hand my project to an installer, I want to trust a person I know, so I do not feel alone against the paperwork.

    What people said

    "Mostly through contacts and recommendations" (#1). "We tell him the file may take long, and we try to be very transparent about it" (#1). "They did it in the agreed time and budget... Congratulations Marc, we will count on you for the next projects" (#6).

The personas

* not in the research. P1 to P5: the pattern each part comes from.

Hard end · A file that is very slow or blocked

Rafael

“I phone every other day to ask, they always tell me the same thing and give me no solution.”

PASS362 wordschecked by check_persona.py

Profile

Rafael*, 48*, owner of a flat in Valencia*. Waiting for a new electricity supply connection. Contacted Voltia through a recommendation*.

Background

Voltia finished the installation and "everything is done perfectly", but the supply is not connected. The legal deadline of 30 working days has passed. He has heard of people waiting 6 months. He does not know the difference between retailer and distributor*.

Goals

  • Have electricity in the flat.
  • Know a date, even a rough one.
  • Stop spending hours on the phone.

Pains and frustrations

  • Every call gives the same answer (P1).
  • Retailer and distributor blame each other (P2).
  • A document is rejected again and again for small reasons (P3).
  • The file may take two weeks or two years, and nobody says which (P4).
  • He feels "desperate" and alone with the paperwork (P5).

Jobs to be done

  • Functional: When the file is stuck, I want to know who has it and why, so I can act.
  • Personal: I want to stop feeling helpless.
  • Social: I want my family* to see that I handled it well.

A day in their life during the wait

Morning: he calls i-DE*, then the retailer*. Midday: he checks his email* for news. Afternoon: he re-sends a document* and writes to Voltia. Evening: he reads forums at night*, looking for people with the same case. The file takes hours every day.

How they look for news today

Phone calls, every other day. Messages to Voltia. Forums, where he asks if he can report them. No one gives him a status; each answer sends him to someone else.

What makes them trust or doubt an installer

  • Trust: the installer picks up, is open about delays, and names a person who follows the file (P5).
  • Doubt: silence, or "it depends" with no reason (P1, P4).
  • He doubts any answer that blames the other side (P2).

Easy end · Follows many files with ease

Andres

“Great professionals... they did it in the agreed time and budget. We will count on you for the next projects.”

PASS374 wordschecked by check_persona.py

Profile

Andres*, 41*, manages several properties* in Valencia. Follows many files at once with Voltia. Came through a recommendation*.

Background

His first project was hard because he had very little time, and Voltia delivered on time and budget. He now sends Voltia every new project in Valencia. Some files take two weeks, so most of his files close fast (P4). He knows the retailer from the distributor*.

Goals

  • Keep many files moving at once.
  • Close each one on the date he planned*.
  • Keep working with the same people.

Pains and frustrations

  • Having to sign papers on time, which he sometimes forgets (P3).
  • A rare file that stays open longer than the others (P4).
  • Phoning for news when a file goes quiet (P1).
  • Not knowing whom to blame if a file stops (P2).

Jobs to be done

  • Functional: When I have many files, I want to see which ones need me, so none stops because of me.
  • Personal: I want to feel in control of my projects.
  • Social: I want my clients* to see that I deliver, and that I chose the right installer.

A day in their life during the wait

He does not wait for one file; he works on all of them. He signs the papers when asked*, answers Voltia quickly*, and calls only when a file is quiet. He talks with Marc*, the person he knows, and not with a call centre.

How they look for news today

He asks Voltia first, by message*. They tell him honestly that a file may take long. He rarely calls the retailer or the distributor.

What makes them trust or doubt an installer

  • Trust: a named person, work done on time and budget, and an honest warning about delays (P5).
  • Doubt: a file that goes quiet with no explanation (P1).
  • Complaints are not followed up at Voltia, so he would not know where to raise one (P5).

Regular · A normal file

Elena

“They are constantly contacting us because these processes take a long time.”

PASS390 wordschecked by check_persona.py

(what the office hears from people like her)

Profile

Elena*, 36*, owns a house* near Valencia. Has one file open with Voltia: an electrical certificate for a small job*. Found Voltia through a friend*.

Background

Her file is normal: most files are active in under 20 days (P4), and hers is somewhere in that range. She has never done this before*. She does not know the retailer from the distributor (P2). Voltia told her the file may take long (P5).

Goals

  • Close the file and have the certificate in her hands.
  • Know roughly when it will be done.
  • Not lose time on paperwork*.

Pains and frustrations

  • She has no news after the first message (P1).
  • She is asked for papers and signatures, and one is missed once (P3).
  • She does not know if "no news" means "all is fine" or "stuck" (P1, P4).
  • She is unsure whom to ask: Voltia, the retailer or the distributor (P2).

Jobs to be done

  • Functional: When I am waiting, I want to know if my file moves, so I can relax.
  • Personal: I want to feel that I did my part and nothing depends on me.
  • Social: I want to tell my partner* the date, without guessing.

A day in their life during the wait

Her life goes on. Once or twice a week* she wonders about the file. If a week passes with no news*, she writes to Voltia. She answers when she is asked for a paper. She signs it late once* and then the file waits for her (P3).

How they look for news today

She writes or calls Voltia, as she has no other way to see the file. She reads the answer: "it may take long". She does not read forums*.

What makes them trust or doubt an installer

  • Trust: the installer was recommended, is honest, and keeps its word (P5).
  • Doubt: a long quiet, because she cannot tell if the file moves (P1).
  • She will judge Voltia by how the wait felt, not only by the result (P4, P5).

What the agent concluded

The agent's report, unedited.

Challenge: clients with an Iberdrola (i-DE) file or an electrical certificate in progress do not know what is happening with it. Details marked with an asterisk (*) in the personas are not in the research. Patterns P1 to P5 are in patterns.md.

What the three share (common jobs to be done)

  • Know where the file is. When the file is open, each one wants to know if it moves (P1).
  • Know how long it will take. Nobody can say if it is two weeks or two years (P4).
  • Not be the cause of a stop. A missed signature or a rejected paper stops the file, and all three depend on small papers (P3).
  • Trust a named person. All three came by recommendation and judge Voltia by honesty and by keeping its word (P5).
  • Know whom to ask. They do not know who does what between retailer, distributor and installer (P2).

The need is the same at all three points: news. What changes is how loud the need is. For Rafael it is a daily emergency, for Elena it is a quiet doubt, and for Andres it is a small gap he fills himself.

Which persona to design for first

Design for Rafael first. He has the strongest and best-documented pain (P1, P2, P3, P4), and four independent sources describe it. If the team understands his case, it covers the doubts of Elena and Andres, which are the same needs with less force. The middle persona, Elena, has the weakest evidence: the research has no direct voice of a normal client, so she is built from the figures of i-DE and from Toni's account, and the team should test her with real clients next.
