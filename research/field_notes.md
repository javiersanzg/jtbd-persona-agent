# Field Research - Team 3 Voltia

*The names of the company, its staff and its client are changed in this public copy (also inside the quotes). Everything else is real and quoted word for word.*

Real sources only. Every quote below is copied word for word from its source (Spanish original, our English translation in brackets). Online quotes were checked against the live page on 23/11/2026; Toni's quotes come from the recorded interview of 05/10/2026.

**Challenge:** Voltia clients with an Iberdrola (i-DE) file or an electrical certificate in progress do not know what is happening with it.

**Method.** Design thinking asks to observe first and then listen, to people at both ends of the line and to an expert. We used three real sources:

1. **Interview** with a user inside the challenge: Toni, admin and sales at Voltia, who answers the clients' calls about their files (WhatsApp voice interview, 05/10/2026).
2. **Online observation (netnography)** of public posts written by people waiting for an i-DE / Iberdrola file, in Spanish electricity forums and a review of Voltia on Google. We read what they wrote, when and how, without taking part. This gives the two extremes of the line in the users' own words.
3. **Expert** voices, published: the CEO of i-DE, the distributor that owns the files in Valencia, and a city official who manages 15 installations, both interviewed by the trade magazine *Energías Renovables*.

## 1. User: Toni, admin and sales at Voltia

**WHO:** Toni, admin and sales at Voltia for a year and a half; he answers clients about their files · **WHERE:** WhatsApp voice interview · **WHEN:** 05/10/2026

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| He answers fast and without notes; on files he goes straight to the problems, not to the process. | «Se atascan mucho, es un jaleo constante, hay que subir mucha documentación» (They get stuck a lot, it is a constant mess, you have to upload a lot of documents) |
| He names two causes that start inside the job, not at Iberdrola. | «A veces el cliente se olvida de que tiene que firmar unos papeles y tienes que ir detrás de él, a veces a los compañeros se les olvida hacer una fotografía y [...] tienen que volver al sitio» (Sometimes the client forgets to sign some papers and you have to chase him; sometimes the team forgets to take a photo and has to go back to the site) |
| Asked how often they go back to a site for a photo or paper: | «Cuatro o cinco veces» [a month] (Four or five times) |
| On what clients ask most: | «Están constantemente comunicándose con nosotros porque son procesos que se alargan mucho» (They are constantly contacting us because these processes take a long time) |
| He cannot give one length for a file. | «Hay expedientes que duran dos años o expedientes que duran dos semanas. Depende de lo que haya que hacer» (Some files take two years and some take two weeks. It depends on what has to be done) |
| On what they tell a client at the start: | «Se le comunica que es posible que el expediente se tarde. Y se intenta ser muy transparente en ese sentido» (We tell him the file may take long, and we try to be very transparent) |
| On who deals with the client: | «Marc es quien más tiene trato con el cliente» (Marc is the one who deals most with the client); clients come «generalmente por contactos y recomendaciones» (mostly through contacts and recommendations) |
| On repeated mistakes, he admits it without being asked twice. | «No se suele hacer un seguimiento de las quejas» (Complaints are not usually followed up) and «en los casos en los que hay algún error que se repite, pues no llevamos un control estricto para evitar que vuelva a suceder» (when a mistake repeats, we do not keep a strict control to stop it happening again) |

**Key takeaway:** The installer is the face of a process it does not control, and part of the delay starts in the job itself (a signature, a photo) without anyone tracking it.

## 7. Expert: Eva Mancera, CEO of i-DE

**WHO:** Eva Mancera, CEO of i-DE, Iberdrola's distribution company · **WHERE:** interview «Las redes son el gigante silencioso», https://www.energias-renovables.com/entrevistas/las-redes-son-el-gigante-silencioso-20240926 · **WHEN:** 26/09/2024

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| She answers with averages, from the distributor's side, about self-consumption (solar) files. | «En 2023 i-DE activó el 59% de los expedientes en menos de cinco días; y el 93% en menos de 20 días» (In 2023 i-DE activated 59% of the [self-consumption] files in under five days and 93% in under 20 days) |
| The slow files are a small share in her figures. | «Tan sólo el 2% de los expedientes gestionados por i-DE se demoraron más de los 60 días» (Only 2% of the [self-consumption] files i-DE handled took more than 60 days) |
| She puts the cause in the process, not in a party. | «Los retrasos eran debidos a la complejidad del proceso» (The delays were due to the complexity of the process) |

## Patterns (verbatim, JTBD, insight)

The full cards, as handed in, are in Tab 1 of the worksheet (`build_worksheet.py`, `PATTERNS`). In short:

| Pattern | Verbatim | JTBD | Insight |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Calls come from silence, not from time | Julio21: «llamo por teléfono día sí y día no [...] siempre me dicen lo mismo»; Toni: «están constantemente comunicándose con nosotros», «dos años o [...] dos semanas» | Functional: know where my file is, what is missing and what comes next | The user cannot tell whether his own wait is normal (two weeks or two years; i-DE's 93% under 20 days is an average for solar files, not his case). Each call that brings back "the same" tells him nobody is looking at his file, so he calls again |
| P2 Nobody owns the file, so the user becomes its manager | pantaleon: «la comercializadora me dice que la distribuidora rechaza [...] y la distribuidora que la comercializadora», «5 horas diarias»; PUMA: «se desentiende de este tema y echa la culpa a Eléctrica Conquense» | Functional: get my file moving without having to coordinate it myself | Retailer and distributor each answer for one piece and send him to the other. Only the user follows the whole file, so he becomes its project manager, for free and at any hour |
| P3 Delays are born on day one and show up weeks later | Tevi62: «la cuarta que los mandan, vuelven a estar rechazados»; Toni: «el cliente se olvida de que tiene que firmar unos papeles»; Herrera: «los propios errores de las distribuidoras [...] empantanar la tramitación» | Functional: have the file accepted the first time | The cause (a signature, a format, an error) and the rejection are weeks apart, so the rejection feels random: the user cannot learn from it and trusts the process less each time |
| P4 Trust is in a person and in a kept promise | N. M.: «con el tiempo y presupuesto pactado [...] Muchas felicidades Marc»; Toni: «Marc es quien más tiene trato con el cliente», «por contactos y recomendaciones» | Emotional (social): keep my word to the people I answer to. Emotional (personal): feel safe because I know one person by name | The happy client praises the kept promise, not the electrical work he cannot judge, and trusts a person, not a company. So a delay is a promise broken by someone he trusted |
| P5 Finished work that cannot be used feels unfair | Julio21: «está todo perfectamente realizado y no puedo tener luz por este trámite»; PUMA: «pasando frío y con la única luz de unas bombillas», «¿Puedo denunciarlos [...]?»; Tevi62: «impotencia» | Emotional (personal): get my normal life back and stop feeling powerless | Once the work is visibly finished, the wait stops looking like part of the job and starts looking like a decision against him: the posts move from "when?" to "can I report them?" to helplessness |
