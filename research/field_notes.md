# Field Notes - Voltia

*Simulated interviews: built from what a team member told us on 05/10/2026 and from how distributor files work.*

**Challenge:** Voltia clients with an Iberdrola file or an electrical certificate in progress do not know what is happening with it.

**Method:** observe first, then a short interview with open questions (no yes/no, past behaviour, exact words). Two extreme users at the two ends of the line (a very slow file and a user who follows many files with ease), one expert, one regular user and one analogous place.

## 1. Extreme user, hard end: a file that took 19 months

**WHO:** Rafael, 58, owner of a small restaurant in Benimaclet (12 tables). Needed more power for a new kitchen · **WHERE:** his restaurant, 16:30, between lunch and dinner service · **WHEN:** Mon 16/11/2026, 45 min

**Questions asked:** Tell us the story of your file from the first day. / The last time you wanted to know how it was going, what did you do? / What was the worst moment of the wait, and why that one? / Who did you tell about it?

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| He keeps a plastic folder behind the bar with every paper of the file. Some pages have coffee stains and handwritten dates on them. | "This folder is my file. I don't trust the emails, they get lost." |
| Before answering the first question he opens WhatsApp and scrolls up a long way to find the first message with Voltia. | "It started in April of last year. Last year. Write that down." |
| When he talks about the waiting he looks at the old cooker, still in use, not at us. | "Every week I asked: is there news? And every week: we are waiting for Iberdrola." |
| He laughs when he says he called Iberdrola himself, but taps the table hard. | "I called Iberdrola once. Forty minutes with music. Then they said they can only talk to the installer." |
| He points at a photo on his phone of a technician on a ladder. | "They came three times for photos. Three. I thought: if they forget the photos, what else do they forget?" |
| He gets calmer when he talks about Marc. | "Marc is a good guy. He always answers. The problem is he never knows more than me." |
| On a sticky note on the till: "Voltia - jueves". | "I put a day to call them, so I don't call every day. Thursday." |
| When we ask the worst moment, he stops for a few seconds. | "When I had to tell my cook that the new kitchen was not coming for summer. She had planned the menu." |

**Key takeaway:** Waiting is bearable; not knowing, and repeating visits for forgotten photos, makes him doubt the whole job.

## 2. Extreme user, easy end: someone who follows many files at once

**WHO:** Laura, 34, project coordinator at the solar panel company that works with Voltia. Follows about 40 files at the same time · **WHERE:** her office in Paterna, at her desk · **WHEN:** Tue 17/11/2026, 35 min

**Questions asked:** How do you follow all your open jobs today? Show us. / Tell us about a file that went fast: what was different? / What do you tell your own client when a file is late? / What makes you trust an installer with the paperwork?

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| Two screens: a shared spreadsheet with one row per client and coloured columns (green, amber, red), and WhatsApp Web open. | "If it is not in the sheet, it does not exist." |
| Each row has a "next step" column and a "who" column with a name, never a company. | "I don't write 'waiting for Iberdrola'. I write who has the ball and what they need." |
| She opens one red row and in 10 seconds tells us what is missing: a signed authorisation from the client. | "Ninety per cent of the delays are a paper that nobody asked for on day one." |
| During the interview her phone buzzes three times; she answers one with a voice note in 15 seconds. | "My clients get a message from me every two weeks, even when there is no news. Especially when there is no news." |
| A printed checklist is stuck on the wall next to her screen: documents and photos per type of job. | "The checklist was born after a bad year. Now the technician cannot leave the site without the photos." |
| She smiles when she talks about the clients. | "They don't want the file faster. Well, yes. But first they want to know that someone is looking at it." |

**Key takeaway:** She calms clients with regular news and a named owner for every next step, even when nothing has changed.

## 3. Expert

**WHO:** Vicente, 49, technical engineer at an engineering office in Valencia that prepares files and certificates for about 30 installers · **WHERE:** video call, his office behind him with folders by year · **WHEN:** Wed 18/11/2026, 30 min

**Questions asked:** When a file gets stuck, what cause do you see most? Tell us the last case. / Which mistakes send a file back? / Which steps can no installer control? / What do you ask for on day one?

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| He shares his screen and shows the distributor's online portal with a list of files and their statuses. | "The client can see a status, but the status says 'in process' for months. It means nothing to him." |
| He counts the most common mistakes on his fingers without looking at notes. | "Wrong or missing client data, the authorisation not signed, photos that do not show the meter or the protection box, and a plan that does not match the site." |
| He sighs when we ask what the installer cannot control. | "If the network needs works, or a permit from the city council, the installer can do nothing. That is when a file goes from weeks to a year." |
| He shows an e-mail template he sends to installers. | "I ask for everything on day one, in one list. Every document we ask for later is two more weeks." |
| He takes a long pause before the last answer. | "The client does not get angry because of the waiting. He gets angry because nobody told him the waiting was normal." |

**Key takeaway:** Most delays come from documents asked for late; the rest (network works, permits) nobody controls, so they must be explained early.

## 4. User: homeowner with a normal file

**WHO:** Pilar, 42, nurse, homeowner in Torrent. Power increase for a heat pump. File of about two months, now finished · **WHERE:** her kitchen, with the new heat pump outside the window · **WHEN:** Thu 19/11/2026, 30 min

**Questions asked:** Tell us how your file went, step by step. / How did you find out what was happening? / When did you feel worried, and why?

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| She shows us the e-mails from the distributor on her phone; most are unopened. | "They send e-mails with a number and a code. I don't know what they mean, so I don't open them." |
| She has Voltia saved in her phone as "Electricista Marc". | "I don't call the company. I call Marc." |
| She works shifts; the fridge has a calendar full of her shifts. | "The problem is not the wait. The problem is that they call me when I am at work, to sign something." |
| She checks the heat pump display while she talks. | "In the end it was fast. But I didn't know it was going to be fast, so I worried for nothing." |

**Key takeaway:** Official messages are not understood; the contact is a person, not the company, and calls at the wrong time are the real friction.

## 5. Observation in an analogous place: a car repair garage during an insurance repair

**WHO:** customers waiting for an insurance repair, and the person at the counter · **WHERE:** a garage in Valencia, reception area · **WHEN:** Fri 20/11/2026, 9:00 to 10:00

| WHAT I SAW | WHAT THEY SAID |
|---|---|
| In one hour, four people call or come in to ask about their car. Three of them only want to know "when". | Counter: "It's with the insurance expert. I'll call you as soon as I know." |
| A whiteboard behind the counter lists the cars by plate number with a magnet: red, yellow, green. | A customer: "Yellow is what? Better than red?" |
| One customer gets a photo of his car on WhatsApp while he waits, and he leaves without asking anything more. | "Ah, they've already taken the bumper off. Fine, fine." |

**Key takeaway:** A visible status and a small proof of progress (a photo) stop the 'is there news?' calls.

## Patterns (for the session 17 warm-up: verbatim, JTBD, insight)

| Pattern | Verbatim | JTBD | Insight |
|---|---|---|---|
| The wait is not the problem; not knowing is | "They don't want the file faster... first they want to know that someone is looking at it" (Laura) | Emotional (personal): feel that my file is in good hands | People tolerate long waits when the waiting is explained as normal and someone shows they are watching it |
| The client trusts a person, not a company | "I don't call the company. I call Marc" (Pilar) | Social: have someone I can count on | Trust sits with one person, so all the news has to go through him, and he becomes the bottleneck |
| Official status messages mean nothing | "The status says 'in process' for months" (Vicente) | Functional: know what happens next and when | A status without a next step and an owner gives no calm |
| Delays come from a missing paper or photo | "Ninety per cent of the delays are a paper that nobody asked for on day one" (Laura) | Functional: get it right the first time | Forgotten photos make the client doubt the whole work, not only the paperwork |
| Small signs of progress calm people | The garage photo on WhatsApp | Emotional: see that something is moving | A small proof of progress replaces the call that asks "is there news?" |
